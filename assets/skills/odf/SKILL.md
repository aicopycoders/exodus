# ODF

There are two ways an ad account grows with creative. Vertical is same people, more to say to them. Horizontal is new people. A segment is not a vibe. It is one outcome, one demographic, and a facet or two: "3AM · women 45 to 60 · on HRT and still". Change any part and it is a different human and usually a different ad. Most accounts slice on outcome and demographic. The gold is in the facets and the sub-outcomes.

The sentence to keep: ads are made of parts, some proven and some cheap to swap, so every launch should be one cheap swap away from something proven. A new segment is the expensive swap. This method makes sure the segment is real before anyone pays for it.

## Think mode, always

This runs as a back-and-forth. One axis per message. Every item one or two words. Propose a list, let the user cut it, move to the next axis. No document until the axis is agreed, and no grid until every axis is agreed. The user makes every call, fast, by name or by number. When they say "stress test", apply the rules and show only what moved. When they say "are we missing anything", check the data for the biggest themes not on the list and name at most three. When they say "note your place", say what is done, what is missing, what is next, then wait.

Research inside an axis (rip these ads, scrape these reviews) is a build burst: say the plan back in two lines, get the go, run it comprehensively, write files early, then come back to the list. Read the `thinking-vs-build` skill if installed.

## The grammar

**Outcome** is the spine. What the buyer wants fixed, one or two words. Each outcome breaks into sub-outcomes through five lenses: type (which kind), location (where it shows), function (what stops working), moment (when it hits), severity (how bad). Function and severity cuts make different humans (dead bedroom versus weak erections). Moment cuts usually make symptom lines, not humans.

**Demographic** is what you could tick from across the room: gender, age band, race or ethnicity, buying for whom, occupation, life stage, medical identity. Include the groups the product is not for yet. Mark exclusions.

**Identity** is who they say they are: the one who holds everyone together, not a pill person, the researcher, gym guy. It is its own axis in the map because the user audits it separately and it is where casting and voice come from. It also lives in Exodus as a demographic row and as a facet family, so on export the same list goes to both.

**Facet** is why they are here, invisible from across the room. Four families in the map: **Belief** (what they think causes it), **State** (a condition, what they are on, what they tried), **Value** (what they prefer), **Occasion** (something that happened; it has a date). Two laws of a good facet: it is self-verifiable in one second ("do you sit six hours a day?"), and it usually carries its mechanism (sitting → blood flow), which half-explains the problem before the ad does. A facet also sets awareness: "just hit menopause" is problem-aware; "tried three cleanses that failed" is solution-aware and needs a failure-explainer, not an intro. Record awareness on the facet; never make it an axis or you count the same human five times.

**Three words that are not the same thing.** A *Belief facet* is the mechanism the market already believes (cortisol, hormones, gut). The *UMP* is the mechanism a competitor sells as its positioning; competitors are grouped by UMP. The *ingredient story* is this product's mechanism per sub-outcome (star ingredient plus at most one partner plus the form factor); it lives in the folder grid and in the copy, not in Exodus. Keep the three apart in every list.

**Persona** is one segment named as a human, with a self-ID line built from their words, a quote bank, and a first transplant.

## Stress-test rules (apply on every axis)

- One or two words per item. If it needs a sentence it is not an item yet.
- Different human, not a synonym. Merge sub-outcomes that describe the same person.
- Nothing in two rows. 3AM lives in Sleep, not in Menopause or Cortisol.
- States and occasions are facets, not outcomes. "On HRT", "doctor shrugged" go under State and Occasion.
- Mechanisms are not outcomes unless the market adopted the word as the problem (cortisol qualifies; its row holds the visible symptoms).
- Life stages can be outcomes when buyers name the stage as the problem ("my peri symptoms"), and also stay as a layer over the other rows.
- **The AI-artifact test.** A phrase in many ads and few human mouths ("2PM crash", "wired but tired", "brain fog") is a copy line, not a segment. Count it in reviews and comments, not in ads. Does it split the market into a different human who needs a different ad? If not, it is copy.
- Competitor coinage ("stress bloat") is not the market's word. Map the plain word.
- Formula fit. Drop outcomes the product cannot honestly touch.
- Refuse new markets in disguise. A sub-outcome that needs new proof is a different account. Keep the outcome, add a facet.
- Compliance-hot words (anxiety, panic, disease names) can be sub-outcomes, not headline outcomes. Mark them.
- Exclusions are not segments. A contraindication goes in the medical list marked as an exclusion.
- Check the other gender and the other ages after every facet list. The user will.

**Evidence when arguing an item:** own winning ad > long-running competitor ad > comment under a running ad > review or Reddit quote > logic. Quote the proof exactly, never summarise it. Mark pure logic HYPOTHESIS. Never hide your own inventions.

## Entry point 1: MAP

Use when there is no agreed grid yet, whether or not winners exist.

1. **Load what they have** and say it back in one paragraph: product and fixed formula, the outcome it sells today, who buys now, what is winning and fatiguing, the sister SKUs it must not collide with, and the market's words (own ads, competitors' longest-running ads, comments, reviews, Reddit). Never edit a quote.
2. **Outcomes.** Ask for their starting list. Sort each item into outcome, Belief facet, demographic, or copy line. Propose additions from the data. Get to six to ten.
3. **Sub-outcomes.** Three to five under each, through the five lenses. Stress-test. Then "are we missing anything" against the biggest themes in the data (energy and fatigue is the usual one).
4. **Focus order.** Two or three lanes with a simple argument from ads, competitors, reviews, and logic. The user decides.
5. **Demographics.** Gender, age bands, race, buying for, occupation, life stage, medical with exclusions.
6. **Identity.** From comments, reviews, the brand's own cast, and the ads with the best hold. Include the other gender's versions.
7. **Facets by family.** Belief, State, Value, Occasion. Sources: reviews ("what I tried", "what I replaced"), Reddit, comments, competitor ads (what they name and disqualify), logic (the conditions the category obviously has: shift worker, on a GLP-1, on an SSRI, postpartum). Each facet gets its one-second self-ID line and the mechanism it carries. Check both genders.
8. **Ingredient story** per sub-outcome, if the user wants mechanisms in the grid: star ingredient in every one, at most one partner, plus the form factor. Supporting cast never leads.
9. **Full list**, then the SKU collision check, then write the grid (template below), then the three homes: folder, Exodus, shareable.
10. **Segments seen.** Keep a running list in the folder of every segment spotted in a competitor ad or the brand's own ads that is not in the grid yet ("that's always a segment too"). Add to it in every session.

When ads exist, tag every ad, theirs and competitors', to one cell by its net impression. Shaded cells are taken, empty cells are gaps. Rank gaps by size, urgency of the outcome, who is already there, and zeitgeist.

## Entry point 2: PERSONAS

Use when the grid is agreed and the user wants humans to write for. Personas live in Exodus; the folder keeps the quote banks.

1. **The tiered list**, as one numbered chat message. Every segment worth a persona in three tiers: Tier 1, you should have these (proven on two sides, or the obvious missing column); Tier 2, recommended (real signal on one side); Tier 3, could do (hypotheses, cheap to test small). Name the human, not the outcome: *HRT-and-Still Helen (Sleep → 3AM · women 45 to 60 · on HRT and still)*. Each line: name, coordinate, proof in a phrase, what changes in the ad. The user answers by number.
2. **The persona card** for each one picked. Short, because it has to fit the dashboard:
   - Name (the human)
   - Coordinate: outcome → sub-outcome · demographic values · identity · one or two facets
   - Awareness the facet sets
   - One-second self-ID line, in their words
   - What they tried that failed, and what they will not hear
   - Ingredient story for this cell
   - First transplant: which existing winner to aim at them, and the only things that change
   - Quote bank: five to ten exact quotes with source, kept in the active brand's folder at `<brand>/state/personas/<slug>.md`, referenced from the card
3. **Push to Exodus.** Export first, then add personas to the contract, then import.

## Entry point 3: HORIZONTAL

Use when a winner exists and the job is new people. Do not go horizontal too early: if two segments are printing, squeeze them first.

Aim every existing winner at every new persona. Mad Libs, not rewrites. The winner's skeleton transfers word for word: sentence count, line breaks, rhythm, beat order, mechanism, proof, offer, CTA, certainty. Only the segment payload moves: the self-ID line, the symptoms and scenes, what they tried, the words from the quote bank. Most sentences of a good pass are untouched. Never add a beat, an explainer, a risk line, or an urgency line the winner does not have. Never soften a line. Then ten extra hooks per swipe in the persona's own words. A swipe that wins validates the segment. A swipe that loses says the segment is real but needs its own ad, or is not real.

Hand the swipes to the writing pipeline; this skill does not write finished ads.

## Exodus

The dashboard map is Plan → Map. The CLI is the edit surface and the JSON contract is the document:

```
npx @aicopycoders/exodus brand current                       # right brand first
npx @aicopycoders/exodus segment export --out segments.json   # pull the current map (empty is fine)
# edit segments.json
npx @aicopycoders/exodus segment import segments.json          # dry-runs, prints changes
npx @aicopycoders/exodus segment import segments.json --yes    # only if it would delete or archive
npx @aicopycoders/exodus segment show                          # verify
npx @aicopycoders/exodus segment personas --json               # saved personas
```

Rules of the contract, learned the hard way:

- Import is a full replace matched by name. Names missing from the file get deleted; personas they orphan get archived. The CLI refuses without `--yes` if anything would be deleted.
- The dashboard keeps exactly five demographic rows: Buyer, Gender, Age, Race / Ethnicity, Identity. Extra rows are dropped silently. Fold life stage into Age values, occupation into Identity, medical into the State facet family.
- Facet families in Exodus are Belief, Identity, State, Value, Occasion. Send the Identity axis to both the Identity demo row and the Identity facet family.
- There is no ingredient-story slot. Mechanisms stay in the folder grid.
- The persona object shape is not documented in `segment --help` and exports empty on a fresh map. Before the first persona push, export, look for an existing persona object, and if there is none, write one persona with `name`, the coordinate slugs (`outcome`, `sub`, `demos`, `facets`), and a `notes` field holding the self-ID line and first transplant, then let the dry-run tell you what the server accepts. Log the shape in tool feedback once known.

## Grid template (folder copy)

```
# <Brand> — <Product> — Segment Grid (v<n>, <date>)

## OUTCOMES (spine)
| # | Outcome | Sub-outcomes |

## DEMOGRAPHICS (tick from across the room)
| Gender | Age | Race | Buying for | Occupation | Life stage | Medical (exclusions marked) |

## IDENTITY (who they say they are)
<a> · <b> · <c>

## FACETS (why they're here)
| Belief | State | Value | Occasion |

## INGREDIENT STORY (star in every one; at most one partner; form factor)
| Outcome | Sub-outcome | Lead ingredients |

## FOCUS ORDER
1. <outcome → sub · demographic · facet> — one-line argument

## SEGMENTS SEEN (not in the grid yet)
<a> · <b>

## COVERAGE GRID (persona names; blank = gap)
| Outcome | <demo col> | <demo col> | … |
```

## Worked example: Evenkeel Reset Gummies (fictional)

Saffron, magnesium glycinate, rhodiola, evening gummy. Founder a former ER nurse. Sister SKUs Night Tea and Glow Collagen. Twelve tested ads, best hold on a 3AM-waking ad, "stress" ads fatiguing.

**MAP, pass 1, outcomes.** User's list: stress, sleep, cortisol, menopause, mood, energy. Sorted: stress is a Belief facet and a copy line, not an outcome (three human mouths in 400 posts; "3AM" in 115). Cortisol stays (the market adopted the word). Menopause stays as an outcome and a layer. Agreed: Sleep, Mood, Cortisol, Menopause, Energy, plus Cravings from the review data.

**Sub-outcomes, Sleep.** 3AM · Racing mind · Light sleep · Hangover. "Can't fall asleep" dropped: that is Night Tea's lane. Stress test moved "night sweats" out of Sleep into Menopause.

**Focus order.** 1. Sleep → 3AM · women 45 to 60 · on HRT and still (own best ad, competitor Dawnfield running the 3AM angle 140 days, 115 quotes). 2. Mood → Rage · the peri woman. 3. Cravings → Night hunger, as a stack with Glow Collagen. Hold: Energy (rhodiola is a morning herb), Cortisol belly (collagen owns it).

**Facets, State.** On HRT and still · Melatonin fails · Magnesium stopped · Wine to sleep · Off an SSRI (exclusion, serotonergic herb). Self-ID line for the first: "Are you on HRT and still up at 3?" Mechanism carried: HRT fixes hormones, not the night surge. Awareness: solution-aware, needs a failure-explainer. Other-gender check added: men 45+ who wake at 4AM, HYPOTHESIS.

**PERSONAS, tiered list.** Tier 1: HRT-and-Still Helen (Sleep → 3AM · women 45 to 60 · on HRT and still). Tier 1: Melatonin-Fails Marie (Sleep → 3AM · women 45 to 60 · melatonin puts her down not keeps her down). Tier 2: Rage-in-the-Car Renée (Mood → Rage · women 45 to 60 · snapped at the kids). Tier 3: 4AM Frank (Sleep → 3AM · men 45+ · HYPOTHESIS). User picks 1 and 2.

**Card, Helen.** Self-ID: "on HRT and still wake at 3am every night." Tried and failed: HRT, magnesium, a sleep tea. Will not hear: "manage your stress". Ingredient story: saffron + magnesium glycinate, evening gummy. First transplant: the 3AM ad, with the opening line swapped to the HRT self-ID and the "tried" beat swapped to HRT and magnesium; every other sentence untouched. Quote bank in `state/personas/hrt-and-still-helen.md`.

**HORIZONTAL.** The 3AM winner is aimed at Helen and Marie. Two swipes, same skeleton, two payloads, ten hooks each in their words. Marie's swipe wins; Helen's does not. Helen is real (115 quotes) but needs her own ad with the failure-explainer up front. That goes to the writing pipeline as a brief.

## What this skill is not

It does not choose formats, styles, or conceits. It does not write finished ads. It does not decide the mechanism story on its own; that is the `positioning-spiral` loop calling this skill as its targets station and the `copy-instincts` skill for the story itself.

## Standards

Before producing anything, read `STANDARDS.md` at the workspace root (and the section for this output type). Grade the draft against it before showing it; rewrite failures first. When the user reacts with a judgment, a banking phrase, or the same note twice, hand the reaction to `remember-that`. At the end of the session, `handoff` scrapes the chat and proposes entries.
