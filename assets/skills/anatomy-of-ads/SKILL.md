# Anatomy of an ad

Every ad is the same set of parts. Once you can see the parts, you can write a brief by filling them in, dissect anyone's ad by reading them out, and find patterns by counting each part across a set instead of squinting at whole ads. This skill is the parts list and the tests for each part.

Think mode for a read or a brief (lists, one layer at a time). Build mode for tagging a batch. Read `thinking-vs-build` if installed.

## The grid

```
MESSAGING
  WHO   = Segment / Persona
  WHAT  = Concept / Angle (the core idea, the point of the ad)
          Mechanism (UMP, UMS, or both)
          Copy Blocks (what's present, what's added, what's missing)
          Copy Beats (from the beat map; added or missing)
  HOW   = Hook
          Structure (format)

CREATIVE
  Static = Native · Template (many types) · Branded
  Video  = Conceit (what media it pretends to be) · Actor(s) · Style · Terrain · Setup
```

The same parts as a stack, top to bottom, each layer constraining the next: **Market** (segment, persona, awareness) → **Substance** (concept, mechanism, proof) → **Frame** (the angle) → **Structure** (the skeleton, an ordered set of beats filling block roles) → **Rendering** (medium, then conceit, actor, style, terrain, setup) → **Surface** (the literal words and pixels; the hook is the top of it).

## The rule that orders the stack

The deeper a part sits, the less meaning it carries and the cheaper it is to swap. A style swap changes nothing the buyer has to believe. A format swap changes delivery, not the answers. An angle swap changes the doorway, not the claim. A concept swap is a new bet. A segment swap is the most expensive bet on the board.

Same list read as what kills you if wrong: segment and mechanism are driver genes, get them wrong and you are at zero; style is cosmetic, get it wrong and you are at minus thirty percent.

So: every launch should be one cheap swap away from something proven. Luke's order of what to play with most, for video especially: segment, format, style, conceit.

## The layers, with their tests

### WHO: segment / persona

Three parts, ODF: Outcome (what they want), Demographic (what you could tick from across the room), Facet (why they are here: belief, state, value, occasion). One outcome × one demographic × a facet or two. Change any part and it is a different human and usually a different ad. The facet is the underrated axis: self-verifiable in one second and usually carrying its own mechanism ("do you sit six hours a day?" → blood flow). A persona is the segment with its evidence attached: the verbatim quotes, the awareness the facet sets, the failed solutions in their language. Awareness rides on top; it is not inside the segment. Depth: `odf`.

Tag test: could you say who this ad is for in the ODF shape from the ad alone? If it only names a demographic ("women over 40"), the facet layer is empty; that is the usual gap.

### WHAT: concept / angle

Concept is the raw truth, the flat fact, the WHAT. Angle is the psychological frame wrapped around it, the WAY IN. Tell: if you can say it flat, with no villain, no feeling, no "you", it is a concept. One fact, "vitamin D deficiency is linked to depression", through five angles: conspiracy ("why your psychiatrist will never check your vitamin D"), geographic ("why almost nobody in sunny Dubai gets seasonal depression"), accidental discovery ("a lab tech's mistake revealed what vitamin D does to serotonin"), countdown ("the FDA is moving to restrict high-dose vitamin D in 30 days"), enemy admission ("even the people who make antidepressants quietly take this").

The precise version: a concept is a causal diagram, not a sentence. Same graph, same concept, even if the words differ. An angle is which role in that graph you star: star the adversary and it is a conspiracy ad; the discoverer, a breakthrough ad; the experiencer, a story ad; the clock, a warning ad. The affordance rule: an angle is available only if a fact fills its role. No admitting insider, no enemy-admission ad.

Two asymmetries. Concepts burn out ("cortisol belly" saturates); angle families do not (conspiracy as a frame never wears out). You have a handful of true concepts and almost unlimited angles, so most firepower goes into angles. And why split them: if timeline ads keep winning, the market is responding to the angle; if "silver" keeps recurring across stories and conspiracies alike, it is responding to the concept.

Angle families: Discovery · Urgency · Authority · Conflict · Story · Reframe. Full list, the affordance roles, and the insight-vector operations that generate concepts: `references/concept-angle.md`.

### WHAT: mechanism (UMP, UMS, or both)

UMP is the hidden reason behind the problem ("why were you able to figure out this hidden cause when other experts haven't"). UMS is the unique solution and how it was found ("how did you uniquely discover this when everyone else missed it"). The concept is the destination (the one-sentence edit: "your bloating is parasites"); the mechanism is the route that gets the edit accepted. The modal shape in winning ads is the re-diagnosis: relabel ("it's not fat, it's sludge"), install the hidden cause, sweep the retrodictions (it must explain their symptoms, every prior failure, and why it is worse now), draw the edge to the solution, gate it with a verification ritual.

The brand gate is the beat the beat map does not have and the winning corpus runs most consistently: only this version of the solution reaches the cause. Generic is dead → sourcing specifics → a way to verify. Tag it in or beside the mechanism field. Naming: body thing plus what it is doing wrong, and the name must imply the fix. Depth: `mechanism`.

### WHAT: copy blocks

Six: Pain, Promise, Proof, Curiosity, Constraints, Conditions. Rate each present block 1 to 10 and name what is missing. The rule that matters for added/missing: curiosity is the only block unique to the product, because it holds the mechanism; the other five belong to the market and every competitor can say them. No curiosity block, no engine. Test for a line: paste it into a competitor's ad; if it still works, it is grey. Depth: `copy-instincts`.

### WHAT: copy beats

The beat map is fourteen narrative beats reverse-engineered from VSLs, each defined by the subconscious question it answers. It is the story of the product, and it is where the point of an ad usually comes from: read down the beats and one of them is the ad. Short video forces ten more beats into existence that the map does not list: call-out, absolution ("it's not your fault, it's the mechanism", arguably the highest-leverage beat there is), demonstration, future pacing, comparison, offer reveal, CTA, re-hook, qualification, belonging.

Added/missing is a verdict audit: tag every line with the belief it is there to close, then ask which beliefs are left unreached and whether the market's awareness already covers them. Overbuilt shows as bloat (the timing beat is the usual offender); unreached shows as the reason it did not convert (risk is the usual gap). Full map and the nine verdicts: `references/beat-map.md`.

Copy melodies: "more specific psychological moments, lines, or clusters we might add." Only one line of definition exists. The nearest concrete material is the Make moves in `copy-instincts`. Open.

### HOW: hook

The hook is concept plus angle compressed to the fewest, sharpest words. Structurally it is a beat promoted to slot zero, usually the body's organising beat; when hook and body diverge too far you pay the clickbait tax (high CTR, dead conversion). Hooks rotate independently of the body: five hooks on one identical body is a clean experiment, and the way to test is five made maximally different, not five micro-variations.

Craft rules: immediate relevance · charged words (object words and action verbs) · an open loop · stakes · specific and tangible · caveman-simple · consequence first, mechanism later · Germanic over Latinate · one sentence · a 1 to 3 intensity dial · always some wildcards. "Most language is filler, filler, filler, then a point. Be clear what the point is and let the rest be caveman."

For unaware traffic, the straddle: curiosity plus relevance in the first line. Relevance has four levels of charge: existential → mechanism → condition → sensation. The onion ladder shows the dial from unaware to problem-aware: what onions do to you → your body → your health → your blood sugar → your neuropathy. Full rules and ladders: `references/hook-and-structure.md`.

### HOW: structure (format)

The word "format" hides four dials. Say which one you mean: **medium** (static, video, carousel), **skeleton** (beat order), **conceit** (what it pretends to be), **style** (visual gloss). On Luke's own list, podcast and 80s retro news are conceits, timeline and specimen VSL are skeletons, UGC is a style and actor choice, song ad is a conceit with a style.

Structure proper is the skeleton: the beat order. A format is a beat promoted to spine, the way a hook is a beat promoted to slot zero, which is why "conspiracy timeline" reads as one thing: the adversary beat is both spine and hook. Skeletons are walks through the concept graph: a timeline narrates it forward, a mechanism explainer walks back from symptom to root, an exposé radiates out from the adversary. Luke's short-ad skeleton: HOOK → POINT → MECHANISM → OFFER. Awareness collapses skeletons: each notch up collapses the middle beats because their beliefs arrive pre-closed. Format beats copy (the Lampropoulos principle): the same copy in a new format has been the massive winner more than once.

### CREATIVE

**Static:** native (looks like a post, a screenshot, a text thread), template (the fifty-odd structured ad types: testimonial, before-and-after, comparison, sign-holder, infographic, and so on), branded (the product is the picture).

**Video:** conceit (street interview, podcast, on stage, doctor visit, customer call, cue cards, news desk), actor (founder, nurse, customer, cartoon, nobody, method-acted prospect), style (UGC, Pixar, claymation, greenscreen, whiteboard, mummies), terrain (where it is shot: kitchen, car, clinic, stage), setup (the situation the conceit needs in place: a phone being shown, a whiteboard already drawn, two chairs). The conceit is the hinge between message and creative: the last decision about how you say it and the first about how you show it. A customer-call script has two voices; a cue-card script is one beat per card. Test for any creative element: does it carry the message, or just host it?

## Three uses

### 1. Tag an ad (the read)

One ad in, one filled grid out. Quote the ad for every field; do not paraphrase.

```
AD: <id / brand / source / run days or result>
WHO      segment (O × D × F) · persona if named · awareness caught at
CONCEPT  the flat fact, one sentence (or "none: a pure angle on a market truth")
ANGLE    family · role starred · the door line, quoted
MECH     UMP (the named cause) · UMS (the named fix) · brand gate present? · name implies fix?
BLOCKS   pain n/10 · promise n/10 · proof n/10 · curiosity n/10 · constraints n/10 · conditions n/10 · missing:
BEATS    in order as they appear (from the map + the ten) · overbuilt: · unreached:
HOOK     quoted · which beat promoted · intensity 1–3 · straddle / symptom / worldview / story
STRUCT   medium · skeleton (beat order in five words) · conceit · style
CREATIVE static: native / template:<type> / branded · video: conceit · actor · style · terrain · setup
SWAP     the cheapest swap that would make this a different ad · the expensive one
NOTE     one line: why it works or why it died
```

For a single winning ad that deserves a full dissection, the `message-isolator` Genesis bot returns an eight-layer message object with exact quotes; run it, then fill the grid from it.

### 2. Write a brief (the build)

Same grid, filled before the ad exists, top to bottom because each layer constrains the next: segment and awareness first, then concept and mechanism, then angle, then the beats the awareness level still needs, then hook candidates (five, maximally different), then skeleton, then the creative choices with the conceit chosen last on the messaging side and first on the creative side. A brief with an empty curiosity row or an unnamed mechanism is not ready to write.

### 3. Feed patterns and gaps

Tag a set of ads with the grid, then count per layer: which segments, which angle families, which mechanism words, which beats, which hooks, which conceits. A pattern in one layer with the others held constant is what tells you what the market is responding to. Hand the counts to `patterns-and-gaps`; it reads them by layer and finds the gaps by layer.

When a definition here is unsettled for your brand (concept versus angle, where the brand gate sits), bank the call with `remember-that` and it becomes the rule here.

## Worked read (fictional)

A competitor ad in the fictional cortisol lane, 289 days running. WHO: cortisol belly × women 45 to 60 × "tried everything" (solution-aware). CONCEPT: night-time cortisol keeps fat locked on the midsection. ANGLE: Authority, discoverer starred: a nurse who "sees it a thousand times a week". MECH: UMP "cortisol lock" (name implies a key), UMS the two-ingredient evening blend; brand gate present ("most ashwagandha is root powder; ours is the standardised extract"). BLOCKS: pain 9, promise 7, proof 6, curiosity 8, constraints 5, conditions 7; missing: a risk line with teeth. BEATS: call-out → symptom stack (problem narrative) → failed solutions → UMP → absolution → UMS → demonstration → timeline → offer; overbuilt: momentum; unreached: risk. HOOK: "You're not fat. Your cortisol is." Beat promoted: absolution. Intensity 3. Symptom hook. STRUCT: video, skeleton = diagnosis-then-timeline, conceit = clinic visit, style = UGC with lower-thirds. CREATIVE: actor nurse in scrubs, terrain clinic hallway, setup a clipboard. SWAP: cheapest = style (shoot it as a phone call); expensive = segment (men 35+). NOTE: works because the absolution beat is the hook and the spine, and the brand gate lands before the offer.

## What this skill is not

It does not choose the segment (odf), find the mechanism (mechanism), or write the ad. It is the parts list every one of those skills refers to, and the read that turns any ad into comparable data.
