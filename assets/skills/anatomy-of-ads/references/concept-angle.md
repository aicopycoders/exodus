# Concept, angle, and where concepts come from

## Concept versus angle

Concept: the raw truth, the flat fact, the WHAT. Angle: the frame wrapped around it, the WAY IN. If you can say it flat, no villain, no feeling, no "you", it is a concept.

Precise version: a concept is a causal graph. "Chronically high glucose → endothelial damage → narrowed penile artery → reduced flow → weaker erections, with a feedback loop shame → avoidance → stress → worse glucose." Two ads on the same topic with different graphs are different concepts. Same graph, same concept.

Angle: which role in the graph you star.

| Role starred | Angle |
|---|---|
| Adversary | conspiracy / exposé |
| Discoverer | breakthrough / accidental discovery |
| Experiencer | story |
| Clock | warning / countdown |
| Admitter (insider) | enemy admission |
| Place | geographic |
| Prior belief violated | reframe / myth |

The affordance rule: an angle is available only if a fact fills its role. Worked on a fertility product: discoverer = the researchers; adversary = an industry with a $25K product to protect; admitter = embryologists who take it themselves; experiencer = a 43-year-old after four failed transfers; clock = the monthly decline rate after 35; place = none found, so no geographic angle; prior belief violated = "old eggs are old, period."

Asymmetries: concepts burn out, angle families do not. A handful of true concepts, unlimited angles; most firepower goes into angles. Diagnostic: if timeline ads keep winning, the market is responding to the angle; if one word keeps recurring across every frame, it is responding to the concept.

Proposed definition, not locked: a concept is a belief they don't have yet; an angle is a belief they already have; the ad is the wire from the second to the first.

## Angle families

Discovery · Urgency · Authority · Conflict · Story · Reframe. The wider taxonomy ran to 55 entries; a stress test kept about 24 and evicted the rest as claims, spines, or proof modes wearing an angle's name (root cause → concept layer; hero's journey → skeleton; quiz → beat; permission granting → the absolution beat).

## Where concepts come from: insight vectors

An insight vector is an edit to the causal graph the prospect already holds. Information adds nodes and needs source trust. Insight rewrites edges between nodes they already have and needs no trust at all, because every node in the rewrite is already theirs. The "aha" is that edit executing in their head.

Eight families, with one example each:

- **Causation shifts**: reverse ("your dog isn't misbehaving because they're stubborn; they're stubborn because they're misbehaving"), hidden, multiple, circular, delayed ("the fat you fight today was programmed by metabolic damage two years ago"), threshold.
- **Feedback loops**: vicious ("each sleepless night floods you with stress hormones, so your mind races faster the next night"), virtuous, balancing, reinforcing, delayed, cross-system ("slouched shoulders become salary problems").
- **Structure revelations**: hidden constraints, system boundaries ("you obsess over kibble brands when the microbiome is the system that matters"), leverage points ("a 3-second adjustment at first crack beats $2000 of roasting equipment"), bottlenecks, dependencies, hierarchies.
- **Context shifts**: environmental, rule, paradigm ("it's not about more DHT, it's about follicle sensitivity"), time horizon, scale, network.
- **Pattern recognition**: hidden cycles, archetypes, fractal, leading indicators ("a 23% spike in one-way U-Haul rentals has preceded every real-estate boom by eight months"), pattern breaks, meta-patterns.
- **Mental model updates**: false assumptions, missing variables, wrong metrics ("amateurs celebrate win rate; pros watch risk-adjusted expectancy"), model limits, model conflicts ("one foot on the gas, one on the brake"), model evolution.
- **Optimisation shifts**: local vs global, visible vs hidden, direct vs indirect ("nagging creates instant resistance"), simple vs complex, static vs dynamic, short vs long term.
- **Relationship revelations**: non-linear, emergent, counter-intuitive links ("your moisturiser is causing premature aging: the moisture dependency effect"), hidden dependencies, synergies, trade-offs ("the harder you cut calories the more your metabolism slows").

They stack. The textbook example: "It doesn't matter if you feed the most expensive kibble or table scraps; all that matters is what their gut converts it into. Think of it as a currency exchange." Zero new facts, one new edge, and the prospect supplies the confirming evidence from memory.

The nine operations every vector reduces to: install node · install edge · sever · redirect · flip · relabel · reweight · retime · swap.

Validation filters: not obvious · reveals a mechanism · proof-free (self-evident) · retellable · suggests an action. Delivery shape of one insight: familiar situation → pattern break → mechanism reveal → "therefore" bridge → action path. Banned: gravity, momentum, snowball metaphors; "success breeds success"; vague energy or force analogies.
