# The instincts catalogue

Each instinct is a question, the reason it works, and a ten-minute practice. They do not fire one at a time. On a real product they fire in parallel in the first minute or two. Practising them one at a time is how they become parallel.

## Meta

**The meta-instinct.** You see every product as a universe of emotional and narrative potential, and you look for the most intense, most coherent, most defensible version of that universe, then build it so completely the reader cannot find the exit. The student looks at the product. You look through the product into the world of pain, desire, identity, and biology around it. Nothing about a product is boring if you go deep enough.

- Psychology over facts (do not be a nerd)
- Commit to one idea
- Probabilities, not truth; the market is the only truth
- Interoception and metacognition: feel the ad before analysing it
- Push to the extreme, then walk back

## See (perceptual)

**Body scan.** "What did that do in my body?" Thoughts, feelings, emotions, sensations. Boredom means the idea is not interesting. Confusion means the mechanism is not concrete. Annoyance means the character is not sympathetic. Nothing means the copy is dead. Practice: read any ad out loud at normal speed, note where your energy dropped, do not analyse until you have the notes.

**Copy blocks.** "Which of the six is this line, how strong, and what is missing?" Pain, Promise, Proof, Curiosity, Constraints, Conditions. Curiosity is the only block unique to the product; it holds the mechanism. Practice: colour a headline, rate each block 1 to 10, rewrite the weakest block only.

**Grey versus colour.** "Could a competitor say this exact line?" Grey is boilerplate, colour is specific and owned. Practice: highlight one ad in two colours; count the colour lines; if under three, the ad has no engine.

**Title and rate.** "If I had to name the idea this is built on, what is it, and how good is it out of ten?" Practice: title five ads in your feed and rate them. The rating gap is your work.

**Idea count and thematic unity.** "How many ideas, and do they belong to the same movie?" Practice: list every idea in a long advertorial, sort into genres, pick the one genre worth building.

**Most interesting line at the top.** "Is the best thing buried?" Practice: find the strongest line in any ad and rewrite the open with it.

## Attract (attractor)

**The north star.** "Infinite budget, infinite time, I am the creator not the marketer: what would have to be true about the world for this to be the best possible solution?" Then argue for that worldview. Practice: for any product, write the worldview in five bullet points before writing a single claim.

**Mine the inherent narrative.** "What is already fascinating about this thing before I write a word?" Interrogate every ingredient, component, origin, process. Practice: pick the most boring ingredient, ask the model where it comes from, who discovered it, what extreme conditions produce it, until one fact makes you say "wait, really?".

**Sympathetic magic.** "What does the source naturally do that maps to the benefit?" Bull is strong, eat bull. Bee pierces, propolis penetrates cells. Salmon regenerate, PDRN regenerates skin. Practice: three ingredients, one sentence each on what the source does in nature, one sentence mapping to the body. The most vivid wins the star role.

**The differentiation hawk.** "What is the one thing no competitor can claim?" Pick the most differentiating ingredient, not the most effective. Practice: list every claim from three competitor ads, then find one true fact about your product none of them mention.

**Preeminence.** "How is it the most, the best, the first, the only?" Practice: write five "the only" sentences about the product and keep the one that is true.

**Associations and magical thinking.** "What does the word already carry?" Rank the fish: caviar, salmon, trout, tilapia. Korean cherry salmon. Norwegian versus farmed. Practice: for the star ingredient, list ten places or types it comes in, pick the one with the richest associations, check if it is true.

**Spokesperson extraction.** "What has this specific person seen that nobody else in the market has?" The photographer thinks in lenses. The CIA agent has classified knowledge. The Norwegian embodies measured science. Practice: write the one credibility sentence only this person can say.

**Format as story.** "Is the delivery mechanism the differentiation?" Drops not chews. Strip not pill. Patch not cream. Practice: name the category norm, write a two-sentence pitch where the format difference is the whole story.

**Existing conversation.** "What is the market already arguing about, and how does this product enter it?" Seed oils, cortisol, chlorine, microplastics. Practice: find the three most charged topics in the market this week and connect the product to each in one sentence.

**Why now.** "What just changed that makes this urgent?" A bacteria that dies off after 40, a new study, a ban, a season. Practice: write one "why now" sentence; if none exists, create one honestly from new research or cultural timing.

**Rotate the dials.** "Which dimensions rhyme?" Ingredient, mechanism, delivery, visual metaphor, spokesperson, history or conspiracy, binary, emotional cascade, packaging, zeitgeist. Spin one, check the others immediately. Chase a rhyme into every dimension. Then find the anti-rhyme, which gives you the enemy and the before state.

**The lock test.** "Can I say the whole thing in one breath, with every clause implying the next?" No "and also". The spokesperson irreplaceable. The proof a chapter of the story. Pain and solution share a root metaphor. See `locked-examples.md`.

**Reification.** "What is this condition called?" Name it: body part plus dysfunction. Pack abandonment syndrome, follicle strangulation, night cortisol. The name becomes a handle, a recurring character, and a search term nobody else owns. Practice: name the mechanism in two or three words and check the name implies fixability.

**Characterisation implication.** "Does the name of the problem contain the seed of the solution?" Dirty can be cleaned. Off can be switched on. Leaking can be plugged. Cannibal, mutated, degenerating imply despair, and despair does not sell. Practice: write the problem name, ask if a stranger hearing only that name would believe it is fixable.

**The binary.** "What are the two states with nothing in between?" Dead versus alive. Horse pills versus targeted strip. Sealed versus penetrated. The bad side must sound like where they are now; the good side like after. Practice: force the mechanism into one visceral word per side.

**The one mental image.** "Can I draw it on a whiteboard?" The whole game of health supplements is one intuitive picture. Green body, red brain at 2PM. Bee stinger piercing a cell wall. Practice: draw the mechanism; if you cannot, simplify until you can.

**Identity and archetype.** "Who does the buyer become, and who do they refuse to be?" Prepared parent versus grieving parent. Measured European versus stressed American. Practice: write both sentences: "people who buy this are the kind who…" and "people who don't are stuck being…".

## Make (generative)

**One idea.** "What is the single most interesting thing here?" Too many ideas or a bad idea are the two ideation mistakes. Practice: cut an ad to the one idea and delete everything that serves another.

**Relevance.** "Does it hit what they actually care about?" The money symptom by name. Practice: rewrite a hook so the consequence named is the one they would pay anything to erase.

**Contrast.** "Where is the good thing bad, the bad thing good?" 3x3 matrix: good / neutral / bad thing against good / neutral / bad claim. Live in the two corners. Practice: list the market's good things and bad things, ten each, then write one flip for each.

**Visual and concrete.** "Can I see it and feel it?" Termites, not bulldozers. Two images, not "different". Practice: take an abstract claim and write the picture a ten-year-old could draw.

**Simplicity.** "One cause, one effect, in three seconds?" Simple, intuitive, new. Domino not three pillars. Practice: reduce a six-part mechanism to two links.

**Intensification.** "How extreme, both directions?" Hell and heaven. Keep asking "and then what?" until you reach biology, evolution, or identity. Practice: five "why is this bad?" in a row; three "and what does that lead to?" for the benefit.

**Pain to bedrock.** "What is the deepest, most primal version of this pain?" Dog barks → pack animal → never alone in 15,000 years → survival panic → cortisol → you torture the creature that loves you most, every morning. Practice: drill any surface pain five levels down.

**Benefit cascade.** "And what does that lead to?" Three cascades down you are in identity and relationship territory. Practice: primary benefit, then three "and that means…".

**The ratchet.** "What gets worse and cannot be undone?" Practice: write the point of no return for the problem.

**Inversion.** "The thing they think is helping is making it worse." Probiotics feed the sludge. Melatonin trains the brain to stop producing melatonin. Extinguishers spray chemicals over your kitchen. Practice: take the number one thing the market does now and find one credible way it backfires.

**Cause-effect flip.** "What if the assumed cause is the effect?" Not rich then women; women then rich. Practice: flip one assumed causality and find one supporting fact.

**Metaphor and figuration.** "What is it like?" All language is metaphorical; metaphors bypass the analytical mind. Be specific: boa constrictor, not snake. Practice: three metaphors for the mechanism, pick the one you can feel in the body.

**Specificity, CRAVES.** Clear, Relevant, Accurate to market speech, Visual, Expressive, Specific. Practice: take one block and pass it through all six letters.

**Emotional trigger words.** "Which two words carry the whole sentiment?" White beard oil. This white carb. Horse pills. Practice: find the loaded phrase in a market's Reddit threads and put it in the hook.

**The claim ladder.** "How absolute can I say it, and can I walk it back in the body?" Doesn't always work for some → doesn't always work → doesn't work → hurts → hurts by doing X. Practice: five rungs for any claim, pick the highest honest rung.

**The thesis.** "Who wronged them, and what does the betrayal mean?" Practice: write the enemy sentence under any hook.

**Permission slip.** "What do they secretly want to keep doing?" Validate wants, confirm suspicions, wrap in a discovery that makes the outcome plausible. Practice: write the "stop doing X" hook for the thing the market hates doing.

**Forbidden characterisation.** "What forbidden pleasure is this like?" Candy, bread, beer, milkshake, pizza ingredient. Practice: one sentence, "this [forbidden thing] does [benefit]".

**Constraint as feature.** "What is the smallest possible commitment, with a number?" 7 minutes, 3 moves, one pill. Practice: minimum effort times maximum result, as a hook.

**Time multiplication.** "Multiply the passive mechanism by duration." 8 hours a night, 365 nights. Practice: one sentence on total accumulated exposure.

**Recursive intensification.** "One more layer down." Name it, then name its stages, symptoms, what happens untreated. Add the number, the date, the study. Practice: three layers deeper on your best mechanism sentence.

**Absurd visual.** "What image is so weird and specific they cannot scroll past?" Obedience juice. Coca-Cola on eggshells. Relevance comes after attention. Practice: the strangest possible visual someone could meet while learning about the product.

**Reverse-engineered evidence.** "Where do I need them to end up emotionally, and what true facts take them there?" Research with intent finds what undirected research misses. Practice: write the target emotion in one sentence, then search.

**Historical excavation.** "What suppression, scandal, patent fight, or ban exists for this ingredient?" MIT melatonin patent. Fake honey wars. Fluoride bans. Practice: find one fact with a date and write it as a hook.

**Narrative worldview.** "Is every sentence load-bearing?" Full gas, no neutral space, everything over-determined. Practice: write the one-paragraph world summary where the problem is the most important thing on earth.

**Framing gravity.** "Does the product feel discovered, not invented?" Tell it as a scientific discovery at dinner; the product is the application. Practice: pitch without the words product, buy, offer.

**Zero tolerance.** "Does this sentence earn its place?" Practice: cut any section by 30% without losing an idea.

## Protect

**Adversarial simulation.** "Where does the most sceptical Redditor bail?" Practice: one hostile thought per paragraph; fix where they turn.

**Cold scroll test.** "Does this line make sense to someone who has only read the lines above?" Practice: read from line one as a stranger; mark the first line that needs context it was not given.

**Body scan on review.** "What did my gut do?" Same as the See version, run on your own draft.

**Congruence.** "Does every element point the same direction?" Pain matches mechanism. Character matches worldview. Spokesperson matches what is taught. Practice: list hook, story, mechanism, proof, tone, design in a column and mark the one that does not belong.

**Blame placement.** "Am I blaming the product or the person?" Blame the oil, not their hair. Blame the industry, not their discipline. Practice: rewrite any hook that makes the prospect the villain.
