# Worked runs

Five runs of the process at different levels. The first four are Luke and Mario's public teaching examples. The fifth is a fictional cortisol-style supplement so the shape is visible on a category like the ones this workspace handles.

---

## Run 1. The beard oil ad (ad level → hook)

**Draft.** "If you oil your beard daily and it's still dry, you're not doing it wrong, you're using the wrong product entirely. And here's why it's not your fault. The oil you're using, whether it's from the drugstore or Amazon, was formulated for straight hair. Different texture. Different biology. On your hair, it just sits on top and does nothing… Your hair, 4b, 4c texture, curls so tight that the natural oils your skin produces get trapped at the root… So your beard is starving on the outside while your skin is drowning in oil underneath… That's exactly why two brothers from Atlanta built this kit…"

**Frame.** Ad. Black men with beards. Money symptom: patches (not dryness, not acne).

**See.**
- Body scan: reads AI, dashes, mildly bored, one line woke me up.
- Copy blocks: pain present and long, promise thin, proof at the end (100,000 kings, 4,000 reviews), curiosity is the straight-hair mechanism, no conditions beyond "first-time discount".
- Colour: formulated for straight hair · sits on top and does nothing · 4b/4c curls so tight oil can't move · starving outside, drowning underneath · two brothers from Atlanta. Grey: "you've tried everything", "here's why it's not your fault", the cycle paragraph.
- Title: "Your beard oil isn't working." Rated 3 to 4 out of 10.
- Ideas: one, but the best lines are on lines 6 to 12, not line 1.

**Attract (light).** Unique: built for 4c texture by two brothers from Atlanta; no water, no sulfates. Thesis underneath: the beard companies made oil for straight hair and sold it in every Black neighbourhood in America. They don't care about Black men.

**Make.**
- Why the colour line is interesting: strong mental image, contrarian, "starving" and "drowning".
- Relevance: do I care that my skin is drowning in oil? Acne, maybe. If I'm a beard person I care about my beard. So: the oil clogs the follicles that cause growth. Now it hits patches.
- Contrast: "sits on top and does nothing" is neutral. "Clogs the follicles" is good thing bad. Then: "most oil" → "white beard oil". Loaded phrase.
- Claim ladder: most oil is formulated for straight hair and not designed for your hair → most oil doesn't work for your hair → most oil doesn't work for Black hair → most oil hurts Black hair → white beard oil hurts Black hair.
- Visual: two images. Oil moving down a straight shaft versus oil trapped at a coiled root. Then metaphor, specific: curly hair strangles the root like a boa constrictor. Name it: follicle strangulation.
- Blame placement (Mario): blame what the oil does, not their curly hair. The oil turns into the snake.
- Intensification and ratchet: drowning your follicles, making it impossible to grow a beard.

**Rewrite (hook).** "If you're using white beard oil as a Black man, you're probably drowning your follicles and making it impossible to grow a beard."

Alternates from the same idea: "Most beard oil is built for white beards. It feeds straight hair and strangles curly hair. That's why your beard stays patchy." · "The beard companies made beard oil for straight hair and sold it in every Black neighbourhood in America. It nourishes straight beards while choking ours." · "Your genes are fine. Beard oil made for straight hair suffocates curly hair. That's why your beard grows in patches."

**Protect.** Redditor: "racist?" is the bail risk; the payoff is that it is true of the formulation, and the body walks it back with the texture biology. Cold scroll: line 1 stands alone. Congruence: the enemy is the oil, the prospect is absolved.

**Probability.** Hook 2 is more likely than hook 1. Nobody knows until the test.

## Run 2. PDRN micro-infusion (mechanism / big idea level)

**Draft.** A product page: PDRN (salmon DNA), hyaluronic acid, peptides, growth factors, delivered through 24k gold micro-infusion stamps. Brief: "market it."

**Frame.** Mechanism. Women buying anti-aging. Money symptom: visible aging the mirror shows.

**See.** Grey: hyaluronic acid, peptides, growth factors. All good, all sayable by everyone. Colour: PDRN itself (early majority; known but still novel) and the gold micro-infusion delivery. No spokesperson with a story.

**Attract (full).**
- North star: I need a worldview where PDRN plus gold micro-infusion is the absolute best form of skincare.
- Two mechanisms exist for PDRN: receptor signalling, or building block that fills the cell. Building block felt more substantial, we are adding something lost to your cells. Chosen.
- Preeminent: if PDRN must be the perfect solution, what must be true about salmon? Salmon swim upstream, get battered, their skin gets wrecked, and they regenerate. Fish skin heals faster than mammal skin. PDRN is used in wound care. So: salmon have the most regenerative skin of any animal. Plausible argument, not a lie; if it is not defensible, move on.
- Association: farm-raised salmon from the Chinese sea, or Norwegian, or Arctic? There is a cherry salmon grown in Korea. Korean cherry salmon. Cherry reads sweet and beautiful, Korean opens the K-beauty file, salmon keeps the mechanism. Nothing about the fish changed. Ask the brand whether it is true before you say it.
- Delivery: why micro-infusion? The capillaries are stuck; nothing taken internally reaches that layer. Why gold? Most valuable metal for its properties; any other metal would degrade PDRN. So it has to be gold, and it has to be infused.
- Coherence: beauty, gold, luxury, Korea, cherry salmon, regenerative skin, DNA, rejuvenating your cells. Cleopatra. Now there is a movie.

**One breath.** "PDRN is DNA from the most regenerative animal on earth, salmon that tear their skin apart swimming upstream and rebuild it. This one comes from Korean cherry salmon, and it's delivered through 24k gold micro-infusion because gold is the only metal that doesn't destroy it and micro-infusion is the only way past your stuck capillaries into the cell."

**Protect.** Cold scroll: "PDRN" needs one clause of definition on line 1. Compliance: "most regenerative animal on earth" is the rung to check; "fish skin heals faster than mammal skin" is the walk-back.

## Run 3. The freelancer headline (hook / headline level)

**Draft.** "The 3-Pillar System That's Helped 1,000+ Freelancers Go From Struggling to Building Predictable Revenue in 6 Weeks (Not Years). Only 15 Slots Left."

**See.**
- Copy blocks: curiosity = 3-pillar system (rated 3), promise = predictable revenue (4), proof = 1,000+ freelancers (7), pain = struggling (vague), constraint = 6 weeks not years (6), condition = 15 slots. All six present. Not missing blocks; each block is weak.
- Title: "A system helps freelancers earn predictably." Rated 3 to 4.

**Make.**
- "3-pillar" feels like three steps. Simplicity: one step. "Client Domino System" is one specific action that creates inevitable momentum, and domino carries associations of chain reaction, gambling, money.
- "Struggling" → specific: feast-or-famine, cold pitching, dry spells, held hostage by referrals.
- Ratchet on the pain: "Every month you spend surviving feast-or-famine is another month you're falling further behind."
- Proof made specific: 16-year pro; worked with Netflix, Google, Lionsgate.
- Promise made specific: land premium clients, add six figures, predictable $10K+ months.

**Rewrites.**
- "Every Month You Spend Surviving Feast-or-Famine Is Another Month You're Falling Further Behind… The 'Predictable Freelance Business' Blueprint That 1,000+ Members Used to Finally Escape the Panic, Land Premium Clients, and Add Six Figures to Their Income."
- "7-Figure Freelancing Expert Who Worked With Netflix, Google and Lionsgate Reveals… New 'Client Domino System' That's Helping THOUSANDS of Freelancers Go From Feast-Or-Famine To Predictable $10K+ Months (Without Cold Pitching, Job Boards, Or Begging For Referrals)."

Not more blocks; each block stronger.

## Run 4. The melatonin claim ladder (advertorial lead)

**Draft idea.** "Melatonin doesn't always work for some people." True, hedged, boring. Nobody stops for it.

**Ladder.**
1. Melatonin doesn't always work for some people
2. Melatonin doesn't always work
3. Melatonin doesn't work
4. Melatonin hurts sleep
5. Melatonin hurts sleep by making you pee more

Rung 5 is the one you would look at. You will never find it by staring at facts about melatonin. You find it by knowing rung 5 is psychologically stronger, then working back: over-the-counter doses are 10 to 100x the effective amount, the surge wears off mid-night, you wake, you pee, you cannot get back down, and your receptors downregulate over time. Walk it back in the body, not in the hook. If it cannot be made true, kill it and take rung 4.

## Run 5. Evenkeel Reset Gummies (fictional, cortisol-style supplement, ad level)

Fictional brand, fictional competitor, fictional facts. Saffron extract, magnesium glycinate, rhodiola, evening gummy. Founder is a former ER nurse. Sister SKUs: Night Tea and Glow Collagen (which already claims cortisol belly).

**Draft.** "Feeling stressed and wired at night? Evenkeel Reset Gummies combine saffron, magnesium and rhodiola to support healthy cortisol levels so you can relax, sleep better and wake up refreshed. Formulated by a nurse. Clinically studied ingredients. Try it risk-free."

**Frame.** Ad. Women 40 to 55 who fall asleep fine and wake at 3AM. Money symptom: the 3AM wake with a racing mind. Not "stress".

**See.**
- Body scan: nothing. Grey from the first word. "Support healthy cortisol levels" is a phrase from every label in the category.
- Copy blocks: promise (relax, sleep, refreshed, rated 2), curiosity (none; the ingredients are listed, not explained), proof (clinically studied, 3), pain (stressed and wired, 2), constraint none, condition (risk-free, 4).
- Colour: "formulated by a nurse" is the only owned line, and it is buried. Everything else is grey.
- Title: "A calming gummy." Rated 2.
- Ideas: several benefits, no idea.

**Attract (full, because the idea is under 6).**
- Unique candidates: an ER nurse founder; the evening timing; saffron as the star (magnesium is everywhere, rhodiola is a morning herb).
- North star: what would have to be true for an evening saffron gummy made by an ER nurse to be the best possible answer to the 3AM wake? Cortisol is supposed to fall at night. In these women it does not; it spikes around 3AM (a second surge), and that surge is what opens their eyes. Falling asleep was never the problem. Staying asleep is. So the whole category, built on falling asleep (melatonin, Night Tea, sleepy teas), is aimed at the wrong hour.
- Spokesperson: an ER nurse works nights, lives on cortisol, and sees what a 3AM surge does to a body every shift. She would notice the second surge before a researcher would. Irreplaceable, because her shift is the mechanism.
- Sympathetic magic: saffron is harvested at dawn, by hand, from a flower that opens for one day. The most expensive spice on earth, calming by tradition. Weaker than salmon, but it carries "rare, hand-picked, precious". Magnesium glycinate rhymes with "the mineral the night spends".
- Binary: fall-asleep products versus stay-asleep products. Or: night cortisol dropping versus night cortisol spiking.
- Name: the 3AM surge. Night cortisol. Check: "surge" implies it can be flattened. Good.
- Catalog check: Glow Collagen owns cortisol belly and face. Reset owns night cortisol and the 3AM wake. Night Tea owns falling asleep. No collision once the lane is "stay asleep".

**One breath.** "You don't have a falling-asleep problem, you have a 3AM problem. Your cortisol is supposed to fall all night; yours spikes at 3AM and that surge is what opens your eyes. An ER nurse who lived that surge every night shift built an evening gummy with saffron and magnesium to flatten it, so you sleep through instead of waking up wired."

**Make.**
- Relevance: 3AM by name, racing mind, ceiling-staring. Not "sleep better".
- Contrast, good thing bad: "Your sleep tea is why you wake at 3AM." (Fall-asleep products push you down hard and let cortisol bounce back.) Bad thing good: "Skip the bedtime routine. Fix 3AM instead."
- Visual: a cortisol line that should slide downhill all night and instead jumps at 3 like a spike on a heart monitor. The nurse has seen that spike on a screen.
- Claim ladder: sleep aids don't help everyone stay asleep → sleep aids don't fix 3AM → sleep aids don't work after midnight → sleep aids cause the 3AM wake → your sleep tea is causing the 3AM surge.
- Thesis: the sleep industry sold you falling asleep because it is easy to promise, and left you alone at 3AM.
- Permission slip: keep your wine, keep your late dinner; the surge is the target.
- Name and reify: "the 3AM surge".
- Ratchet: every 3AM wake trains the surge to come earlier.
- Constraint: one gummy at 8PM.

**Rewrites (hooks).**
- "If you fall asleep fine but wake up at 3AM wired, your cortisol is spiking in the middle of the night, and your sleep tea is making it worse."
- "An ER nurse figured out why women over 40 wake at 3AM. It isn't stress. It's a cortisol surge that hits while you sleep."
- "Stop trying to fall asleep faster. You already do. Fix the 3AM surge instead."

**Protect.**
- Redditor: "cortisol at 3AM, is that real?" The body has to carry the walk-back (the normal overnight cortisol curve and the early-morning rise). Rung 5 of the ladder ("your sleep tea is causing it") is the one compliance will pull; rung 3 or 4 survives.
- Cold scroll: hook 1 stands alone. Hook 3 needs "3AM" to be understood as a wake, which most of this market will get.
- Congruence: enemy is the category and the hour, not the woman. Nurse founder rhymes with the mechanism. Glow Collagen keeps belly and face. Night Tea keeps falling asleep. Nothing points the other way.

**Probability.** Hook 1 is the highest-probability opener because it names the symptom, the hour, and the enemy. Hook 2 tests the spokesperson lane. Only the account decides.
