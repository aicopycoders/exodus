# Copy Instincts

A copy instinct is a question you have asked so many times it turned into a feeling. The feeling that something on the page is off. The feeling of where the best version lives. The feel for the move that gets you there. Students on the A-lister calls already knew every concept (copy blocks, beat map, unique mechanism, intensification). They still could not fix their own copy, because they were not asking themselves the right questions. Ask the right questions and the right answers show up. This skill is the questions, in order, run on a draft.

Three families, one acronym, S.A.M.:

- **See** (perceptual): can you notice what is on the page and what it does to your body?
- **Attract** (attractor): do you know where the best version lives, the north star you are being pulled toward?
- **Make** (generative): do you know the moves that get you there?

They run at three levels: big idea / mechanism, ad copy, hooks and headlines. Hooks need ideas, ideas need mechanisms, and good hooks reveal mechanisms. Not linear. Find out which level the draft is at, but always check the level above it, because a hook cannot outrun a weak idea.

## The big rules (read before every run)

1. **Psychology over facts.** The best idea is never sitting in the product's facts or the ingredient list. Go find the psychologically interesting idea first, then work back to make it true, then prove it, then soften for compliance. Facts-first produces the same regurgitated lines every model and every competitor already has. Do not be a nerd.
2. **Commit to one idea.** One ad, one idea. One hook, one idea. Fine dining is a few specific ingredients, not a stew. Stop being an idea polygamist. Pick one and let it live or die on that.
3. **Probabilities, not truth.** Nobody knows which version wins before the test. Everything here raises probability. The market is the only truth. Say "more likely" and mean it.
4. **Push to the extreme, then walk it back.** It is always easier to soften an idea than to find a good one. Ideate with the brakes off, then dial back to brand, compliance, and what is honestly true. Most copy dies from being under-bold, not from being over-bold.
5. **You are not selling the product, or the outcome.** Everyone sells the same outcome. You are selling the worldview that makes it true that this product is the only-est, most platonic version of the solution. The product falls out of the worldview.

## The run

Do the steps in order the first time. Report what you found at each step in a few lines, not a document, unless the user asked for the full write-up. The whole point is that the user sees the questions being asked, so they start asking them too.

### Step 0. Frame it

Three things before reading:

- **Level.** Is this a hook / headline, an ad or email, or a mechanism / big idea? Say which.
- **Who.** Who is the market, in one line. If it is unclear from the draft, that is finding number one.
- **Money symptom.** The single most emotionally loaded pain named in the market's own words. Not "dryness" or "skin". For a beard product it is patches. For a menopause product it might be belly, rage, or 3AM. Every idea will be judged on whether it hits this.

### Step 1. See

**1a. Body scan.** Read the draft once at normal speed, cold, as a member of the market who has no other context. Then report thoughts, feelings, emotions, sensations. Where did energy drop? Where were you bored, confused, annoyed, curious? "Feels AI", "em-dashes", "nothing happened" are valid findings. Your gut reaction is the first focus group and the most important diagnostic. Do this before any analysis.

**1b. Copy blocks.** Colour the six: Pain, Promise, Proof, Curiosity, Constraints, Conditions. Rate each block that is present 1 to 10. Name what is missing. Then remember: curiosity is the only block that is unique to the product, because it holds the mechanism. Pain, promise, constraints, and conditions belong to the market and every competitor can say them. If the curiosity block is weak or absent, the draft has no engine.

**1c. Grey versus colour.** Highlight the lines that are interesting (colour) and the lines any competitor could say (grey). Colour is usually specific, visual, contrarian, or a fact only this product owns. In the beard oil ad, "formulated for straight hair", "sits on top and does nothing", "4c curls so tight the oil can't move", "two brothers from Atlanta" were colour. "You've tried everything" was grey. The test for a line: could you paste it into a competitor's ad and it would still work? Then it is grey.

**1d. Title and rate.** Give the draft a title in one line, the way you would name the idea it is built on. Rate that idea 1 to 10. Be honest. "Your beard oil isn't working" is a 3 or 4. Most drafts are a 3 to 5. That number is the gap this run has to close.

**1e. Count the ideas.** One, or several? If several, list them, then check thematic unity: do they belong to the same movie? Ancient remedy (Japanese monks) and new scientific breakthrough (MIT brain cells) are two different genres. Combining genres is possible but hard. Pick one.

**1f. Position.** Is the most interesting line at the top? If the best idea is buried on line 8, the first fix is to move it to line 1.

### Step 2. Attract

Run this fully when the idea rated under 6, when the level is mechanism / big idea, or when the user asks for new ideas from a swipe. Run it lightly (one pass through the north star question) when the idea is already strong and the job is amplification.

**2a. The north star question.** Imagine infinite budget, infinite time, and you are the product creator, not the marketer. What would have to be true about the world for this specific product, with its specific unique part, to be the best possible solution ever designed? Argue for that worldview. This is the attractor. Every idea you consider gets measured against the feeling of "the answer was right there the whole time and nobody saw it".

**2b. What's unique.** List candidates: star ingredient, a specific combination, the creation process, the story of the product, the founder or spokesperson, a quality or proof of an ingredient, the delivery mechanism, packaging, branding, an association, the moment in culture. Science alone is not unique enough. Pick the sexiest, most irreplaceable one. Then twist everything else to line up behind it.

**2c. The five questions.** Ask them in this order about the unique thing:
1. What is most unique about this product that cannot be said about anyone else?
2. How do we make that element preeminent, a category of one: the most-est, best-est, first, only?
3. What associations and magical thinking make it even more pristine? What does the source naturally do in nature that maps to the benefit? Salmon fight upstream, get battered, regenerate their skin. Bees pierce. Volcanic rock forges under pressure and purifies. Bulls are strong. Humans are associative creatures, not logical ones. Rank the fish: caviar, salmon, trout, tilapia. Korean cherry salmon beats farmed salmon, and nothing about the fish changed.
4. How does this specific combination become the perfect combination for this time and place? What is the market already arguing about (the zeitgeist) that this enters?
5. How do we make all of it coherent?

**2d. Rotate the dials.** Hold the dimensions loosely and spin them against each other: ingredient, mechanism, delivery mechanism, visual metaphor, spokesperson, historical or conspiracy narrative, cognitive binary, emotional cascade, packaging, zeitgeist. You are looking for rhymes, where two dimensions say the same thing in different languages. When you find one rhyme, chase it into the other dimensions. Then find the anti-rhyme: the mirror image is your enemy and your before-state (sealed cells versus penetrated cells, dead oil versus living oil).

**2e. The lock test.** It is locked when you can say the whole thing in one breath and the listener nods before you finish. Read `references/locked-examples.md` when you need the shape. Signs it is not locked:
- you keep saying "and also" instead of "which means" or "because"
- the mechanism and the star ingredient feel like separate decisions
- the spokesperson could be swapped for anyone
- the proof is bolted on as a defence instead of being a chapter of the story
- the pain and the solution do not share a root metaphor
- the name of the problem implies it cannot be fixed (cannibal brain) instead of implying the fix (dirty brain can be cleaned, leak can be plugged, switch can be flipped)

**2f. Name it.** Give the mechanism or condition a two-or-three-word name: body part or biological thing plus a dysfunction or action word. Follicle strangulation. Night cortisol. Estrogen leak. Check the name implies the solution is reachable.

### Step 3. Make

Take the one idea and add voltage without changing the truth. Each move is a question. Ask all of them; use the ones that move the idea.

- **Relevance.** Does it hit what they actually care about? If they are worried about their beard, they care about their beard. "Skin drowning in oil" became "clogging the follicles that cause growth", because patches are the money symptom and acne is not.
- **Contrast.** Where is the good thing bad, or the bad thing good? Draw the 3x3: good / neutral / bad thing, times good / neutral / bad claim. Live in the two corners: good thing bad, bad thing good. Every market has a list of good things and bad things; write both lists, then flip. Why sunscreen ages you. Why smoking is great for your skin. Why cortisol is the fountain of youth. "It does nothing" is neutral; "it clogs" is good thing bad.
- **Visual and concrete.** Can I see it? Can I feel it? You cannot see "different". You cannot see "0.7 viscosity". You can see oil moving down a straight shaft versus oil trapped at a coiled root. Cytokines eating your cartilage like termites, not bulldozing it. Interoceptive: "your joints are dry, this pill hydrates them" is felt in the body.
- **Simplicity.** Does a ten-year-old get it in three seconds? One cause, one effect. Simple, intuitive, new. Domino beats three pillars because a domino is one step that creates inevitable momentum and three pillars is three steps.
- **Intensification.** How extreme in both directions? Run the chain down to hell (low T → wife leaves → lose job → homeless → forgotten → kids piss on your grave → next to Hitler in the textbooks) and up to heaven. You will not say it all; it is the undertone you get to suggest. The pain ladder and the core wound live here.
- **The ratchet.** What gets worse and cannot be undone? What happens if you do not fix it in time? The point of no return creates urgency without a discount.
- **Metaphor.** What is it like? All language is metaphorical ("throw an idea at you", "grasp a concept"); metaphors carry the feeling into the nervous system. Be specific with the metaphor: not snake, boa constrictor.
- **Specificity (CRAVES).** Clear, Relevant, Accurate to how the market speaks, Visual, Expressive with charged words, Specific. "Nature's candy that doctors say is why Danish people have low blood sugar" becomes "the bizarre Arctic berry clinical studies prove is why Scandinavians have 3x lower rates of high blood sugar despite stuffing their faces with mountains of carbs".
- **Emotional trigger words.** Loaded phrases carry a whole sentiment in two words: white beard oil, this white carb, horse pills. Hunt for them.
- **The claim ladder.** Write the neutral claim, then climb: "melatonin doesn't always work for some people" → "melatonin doesn't always work" → "melatonin doesn't work" → "melatonin hurts sleep" → "melatonin hurts sleep by making you pee more". Pick the highest rung you can walk back honestly in the body, then walk it back there, not in the hook.
- **The thesis.** Who wronged them and what the betrayal means. The beard companies don't care about black men. The skincare industry was aging you while you thanked it. Blame the product, never the prospect's body (blame the oil, not their curly hair).
- **The permission slip.** Validate what they already want and confirm what they already suspect. A hook that asks them to change their nature loses to a hook that says stop doing the thing they hate. Stop doing cardio if you want to lose the menopause belly.
- **Forbidden characterisation.** Frame the solution as the thing they are told not to have: candy, bread, beer, a milkshake. Pleasure, not medicine.
- **Constraint as feature.** The smallest commitment, with a number: 7 minutes, 3 moves, one pill.
- **Time multiplication.** If it works passively, multiply by duration: an 8-hour spa treatment every night.

Full catalogue of the moves, with the question and a ten-minute practice for each: `references/instincts-catalog.md`.

### Step 4. Protect

- **The hostile Redditor.** Read it as someone who does not want to buy. At each line, where do they bail? Fix that line before it becomes the exit.
- **The cold scroll test.** The reader has only the lines above. Does line 1 make sense with zero context? Does line 5 make sense with only lines 1 to 4? Copy written inside a long session assumes the prospect knows the product. They do not.
- **Congruence.** Do hook, story, mechanism, proof, spokesperson, tone, and design all point the same way? The copper pillowcase built desire around pressure and then sold copper ions. The fire blanket story blamed the mom instead of the system. One misaligned element sinks the rest.
- **Compliance last.** Now walk back the extreme rungs to what brand, law, and honesty allow. Flag the hot words rather than silently deleting them so the user can decide.

### Step 5. Hand back

Use this shape every time. Keep prose short; the value is in the before, the after, and the named move.

```
## See
Body scan: <two lines>
Copy blocks: <present, rated; missing>
Colour: <the interesting lines> · Grey: <the boilerplate>
Title: "<one line>" · Idea rated <n>/10 · Ideas counted: <n>, unity: <ok / clash>

## The one idea
<one sentence> — <why it beats the others in one line>
(if asked for options: top 3 ideas, ranked, one line each)

## Attract (when run)
North star: <one breath>
Unique → preeminent → association → time/place → coherence: <one line each>
Name: <2–3 words> · Binary: <bad state> vs <good state>

## Make
Claim ladder: <rungs> → chosen rung
Rewrite(s):
  <hook / lead / mechanism paragraph>
Moves used: <relevance, contrast, visual, …> one per change

## Protect
Redditor bail points: <lines> · Cold scroll: <pass/fail> · Congruence: <ok / what clashes>
Walk-back for compliance: <which words, suggested softer rung>

## Probability note
<what this raises, what only a test can settle>
```

Then, if the user is running an instinct-transfer system, offer to save the before and after as a pair with a one-line note naming the principle. Edits are training events.

## What this skill is not

It does not position a brand from zero; that loop is `positioning-spiral`. It does not write a batch of finished ads; hand the chosen idea and rewrite to the writing pipeline. It does not replace testing. It makes the questions visible so the user's own instincts get sharper every time it runs.

## Standards

Before producing anything, read `STANDARDS.md` at the workspace root (and the section for this output type). Grade the draft against it before showing it; rewrite failures first. When the user reacts with a judgment, a banking phrase, or the same note twice, hand the reaction to `remember-that`. At the end of the session, `handoff` scrapes the chat and proposes entries.
